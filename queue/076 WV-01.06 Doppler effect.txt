Why a siren changes pitch. A source making ripples at a steady rate, standing still: evenly spaced rings, and listeners in front (F) and behind (B) hear the same pitch. Move it at half the wave speed and each new ring starts further ahead: squashed in front, stretched behind. The gaps become λ(1 − M) and λ(1 + M), so the pitch is f/(1 − M) = 2× in front and f/(1 + M) = ⅔ behind. At M = 0.8 the front pitch is 5×.
