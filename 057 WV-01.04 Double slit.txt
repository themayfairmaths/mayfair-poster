The double-slit experiment in a ripple tank. The wall is u = 0 except in the slits, so each slit becomes a new source and the two ripples interfere: bright where the paths differ by 0, λ, 2λ… Double the gap and the fringes get twice as tight. Add a third slit and the bright beams get sharper - the start of a diffraction grating. Make the middle slit bigger and it wins: the centre beam takes over and the calm fringes no longer reach zero. Thomas Young saw the same pattern with light in 1801.
